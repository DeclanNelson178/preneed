# The law behind this model

**Scope.** Massachusetts only. Cost-protected pre-need funeral contracts only.
Funeral establishment charges only. Nothing here is legal or tax advice.

**How to read this file.** Each section gives the citation, the text, what it
means in plain English, and what the model does about it. Text in quotation
marks came from the source named. Text without quotation marks is a summary.

> **Verification note.** I read these provisions through Cornell LII and the
> IRS website on 2026-08-19. Two mass.gov pages refused my request. Before your
> uncle relies on any of this, check the quoted text against the official
> Mass.gov copy of 239 CMR 4.00 and against his own contract form.

## 1. What kind of contract this model covers

**239 CMR 4.01 — Definitions**

A contract is **cost-protected** when the buyer has "fully funded the Funeral
Trust Account, or fully paid for the Pre-need Insurance Policy or Annuity,
within a specified period of time after the Pre-need Funeral Contract is made",
and the funeral establishment agrees to accept the available funds "as payment
in full for all funeral goods and services."

**Plain English.** Your uncle locks the price. Whatever the funeral costs on the
day of the funeral, the family pays no more. He takes the risk.

**What the model does.** It models cost-protected contracts and nothing else.
Non-cost-protected contracts are out of scope. On those, the family pays the
difference, so there is no funeral-home economics to model.

**Note on the timing words.** The definition says "within a specified period of
time". A multi-pay insurance policy can therefore still be cost-protected,
because the pay schedule can be that specified period. This is why instalment
insurance stays in the model and instalment trusts do not. See section 6.

---

## 2. The itemised statement and the two charge categories

**239 CMR 4.02(5)(d)**

The itemised statement must separate:

1. **Funeral establishment charges** — the goods and services your uncle
   supplies himself. Casket, embalming, staff, facilities, hearse, viewing.
2. **Non-funeral establishment charges**, also called **cash advance items** —
   defined in 4.01 as "costs of goods or services which are provided for a
   Beneficiary in connection with his or her funeral by a supplier or vendor
   other than a funeral establishment licensed in Massachusetts" but paid by
   the funeral establishment out of trust or policy funds. Cemetery plot, grave
   opening, vault, death certificates, obituary notices, clergy honorarium.

**What the model does.** Nothing. Your uncle bills cash advance items
separately to the family at the time of service, so they never enter his
pre-need contracts. The model covers funeral establishment charges only, and
uses one inflation rate.

**Limit you must know.** If he ever writes a vault or a cemetery charge into a
pre-need contract, this model does not cover that contract.

## 3. What he may do with the money before the funeral

**239 CMR 4.08 — Restrictions on Use or Disposition of Funds**

No licensed funeral establishment, and no agent or employee of one, may receive
pre-need funds "for any personal use, payment of the operating expenses of any
funeral establishment, issuance of a loan to any person, as collateral for any
loan, or for any purpose."

All funds made payable to a banking institution must go into a funeral trust
account meeting 239 CMR 4.09, **not later than five business days after the
cooling-off period ends** — or be paid to an insurance company for a pre-need
policy or annuity under 239 CMR 4.10, on the same deadline.

**Plain English.** On a trust-funded contract he keeps nothing at the sale. All
of it goes to the bank within five business days. He cannot borrow against it.

**What the model does.** The trust option starts with the full contract price on
day one and pays him no commission. This is not an assumption. It is the rule.

## 4. Who keeps the surplus and who eats the shortfall

**239 CMR 4.08(6)** — the most important provision in this model.

At the time of death the funds are apportioned between the two categories in
section 2 above, then the surplus or shortfall rule is applied **separately to
each category**:

| Paragraph | Contract type | Category | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.08(6)(a) | Cost-protected | Funeral establishment | "may retain the resulting surplus funds" |
| 4.08(6)(b) | Cost-protected | Funeral establishment | "shall bear the resulting loss and shall not bill the estate" |
| 4.08(6)(c) | Cost-protected | Cash advance | "may retain the resulting surplus funds" |
| 4.08(6)(d) | Cost-protected | Cash advance | "shall bear the resulting loss and shall not bill the estate" |
| 4.08(6)(e) | Not cost-protected | Funeral establishment | "shall refund the resulting surplus to the estate" |
| 4.08(6)(f) | Not cost-protected | Funeral establishment | "may bill the estate of the contract Beneficiary for the deficiency" |
| 4.08(6)(g) | Not cost-protected | Cash advance | "shall refund the resulting surplus to the estate" |
| 4.08(6)(h) | Not cost-protected | Cash advance | "may bill the estate of the contract Beneficiary for the deficiency" |

**Plain English.** On a cost-protected contract he keeps every dollar of
surplus, and he absorbs every dollar of shortfall. He may not send the family a
bill for the difference.

**What the model does.** The whole settlement calculation is one line:

    margin = money available at death − true cost of the funeral at death

Paragraphs (c) through (h) are out of scope: (c) and (d) because cash advance
items are excluded, and (e) through (h) because non-cost-protected contracts are
excluded.

**Why this matters most.** A graded policy that pays 40% of face in year one,
against a contract he has price-guaranteed, produces a loss of roughly 60% of
the case. 4.08(6)(b) means he cannot pass any of it on.

## 5. The trust account

**239 CMR 4.09 — Funeral Trust Accounts**

- **4.09(2)(a).** The trustee must be "a federal or state-chartered banking
  institution which has trust powers, or a trust company, within the
  Commonwealth of Massachusetts."
- **4.09(2).** The beneficiary is the person for whom the goods and services
  will be provided. The funds may be used only to pay for those goods and
  services. "The entire account balance shall be payable to the Licensed Funeral
  Establishment which provides the specified Funeral Goods and/or Services."
- **4.09(3).** Funds "may be invested in any form of investment which may
  lawfully be established or maintained by the trust department" of that
  institution, and "All funds deposited in that Funeral Trust Account shall be
  invested and managed in accordance with M.G.L. c. 203C."

**M.G.L. c. 203C** is the Massachusetts Prudent Investor Act.

**Plain English.** Equities are lawful. The prudent investor rule permits a
diversified portfolio. But the trustee decides the actual policy, and a bank
trust department may still hold the fund in bonds and certificates of deposit.

**What the model does.** The trust rate of return is a user input. The model
does not assume a portfolio. Your uncle must ask the trustee for the written
investment policy and the last ten years of actual return.

**What 4.09 does not say.** It is silent on who pays income tax on the trust
earnings. See section 7.

## 6. Insurance-funded contracts

**239 CMR 4.10 — Pre-need Insurance Policies and Annuities**

- The seller must be "duly licensed as an insurance agent by the Massachusetts
  Division of Insurance", and must comply with all laws governing the business
  of insurance, "including but not limited to all licensing requirements."
- Before accepting funds, the funeral establishment must disclose "that the
  funeral establishment, or its agent or employee, will be paid a commission on
  the sale of the insurance policy."

**Plain English.** He may take the commission. He must hold a Massachusetts
producer licence, and he must tell the family about the commission in advance.

**What the model does.** The commission is a user input. The model shows it as
his income, taxed in the year received. The disclosure duty is a compliance
matter, not a number, so it appears in this document only.

## 7. Income tax on the trust — background only

**The model does not calculate tax.** Your uncle enters one effective rate.
This section exists so he knows which number to ask for and who to ask.

There are two possible treatments, and the trustee chooses between them.

### Treatment A — the trustee makes the qualified funeral trust election

**IRC §685.** The trust becomes a qualified funeral trust, or QFT. Then:

- **§685(a)(2).** "no deduction shall be allowed by section 642(b)" — a QFT gets
  no personal exemption.
- **§685(c).** "Section 1(e) shall be applied to each qualified funeral trust by
  treating each beneficiary's interest in each such trust as a separate trust."

**Plain English.** Each customer's share is taxed as if it were its own small
trust. On a $9,000 contract earning about 5%, that is roughly $450 of income a
year, which sits in the lowest bracket. The effective rate is therefore very
low.

**The trustee may file one composite Form 1041-QFT covering many trusts.** That
is a paperwork convenience only. The IRS instructions say to enter "the total of
the tax figured **separately for each QFT**", and for the net investment income
tax to "treat each beneficiary's interest in each QFT (within the meaning of
section 685) as a separate trust."

### Treatment B — no election is made

Without the election, IRC §671 to §678 apply. The purchaser is the grantor and
is taxed on the trust income personally. Massachusetts follows: **M.G.L. c. 62
§10(e)** provides that where the grantor is treated as the owner under §671 to
§678, the income is taxed to the grantor and is excluded from the trust's
taxable income. In practice the trustee sends the customer a grantor trust tax
information statement.

**Plain English.** The customer pays the tax out of their own pocket, so the
trust balance is never touched by tax at all.

### What your uncle should ask the trustee

The dashboard takes **one** trust number: how fast the account balance grows,
after fees and after tax. So the question is about balances, not rates.

1. Send me ten years of year-end balances for one fully-funded account that had
   no deposits and no withdrawals.
2. Do you make the qualified funeral trust election and file Form 1041-QFT?
3. If yes, send me the last filed return and the matching Massachusetts Form 2.
4. If no, send me a sample grantor trust tax information statement.
5. Send me the written investment policy for the fund.

Answer 1 is the number. Compute `(last balance ÷ first balance) ^ (1/years) − 1`
and type it in. It is correct under either tax treatment: under the election the
trustee pays the tax from the account, so it is already out of the balance;
without the election the customer pays personally, so the balance was never
touched. Either way the balance is what the funeral establishment receives under
239 CMR 4.09(2).

Answers 2 to 4 do not change the arithmetic. They tell him which world he is in,
and they let him check that answer 1 is what he thinks it is. Answer 5 tells him
whether the ten-year history still describes the portfolio the fund holds today.

**Why the model does not ask for a tax rate.** A tax rate needs a base, and the
two obvious bases disagree. Tax divided by income earned and tax divided by
total return give the same answer for a bond account and differ by about three
times for an equity account, because most of an equity return is appreciation
that was never sold and never taxed. A single year is noisy as well, because
gains are realised in lumps. The balance has neither problem.

### Massachusetts rates, for reference

M.G.L. c. 62 taxes fiduciary income at **5%** for ordinary income and long-term
capital gains, and **8.5%** for short-term capital gains. A **4% surtax** applies
to taxable income above a threshold that is indexed each year.

> $1,000,000. The threshold is indexed. Secondary sources report **$1,107,750
> for tax year 2026**. I could not reach the Department of Revenue page to
